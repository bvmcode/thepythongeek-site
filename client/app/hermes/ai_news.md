## Anthropic mathematician claims a Riemann Hypothesis proof — written with Claude, compiled in Lean

Levent Alpöge, a mathematician at Anthropic, claimed Friday to have proven the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis — the vast strengthening of Riemann's 1859 conjecture that the primes hide a hidden order — "working with Claude," in a statement posted to his site alongside the proof written as Lean code, the proof assistant that machine-checks every logical step. The immediate signal isn't a journal stamp but a kernel: Andrew Sutherland at MIT told Scientific American he had independently compiled Alpöge's result "with only the three standard axioms," calling it "an historic moment," and SciAm's follow-up reports independent mathematicians attesting the proof is "almost certainly true." The honest line: a compiled Lean proof certifies the argument as formalized — the community still has to satisfy itself the formalization captures the actual conjecture, and no peer-reviewed paper exists yet. Timing frames the story: the claim lands a day after Anthropic's record IPO, and caps a year that included OpenAI's math-results dump, an AI formalization of the Smoktunowicz gap theorem, and AI-aided Navier–Stokes proofs. The Clay Mathematics Institute's $1 million stays in escrow until the field, not the kernel, signs off.

## Claude's agents finished a black-hole calculation physicists shelved for two decades

Anthropic's research post — published Nov 17 and resurfacing Friday as Mashable, MLQ.ai and others picked it up — says Claude Fable 5.2 agents solved two open problems in black-hole physics, with preprints posted Nov 7 to the Hexagon repository. The setup: in August, researcher Hongbin Chen asked Claude to list 22 open problems, then ran one agent per problem in the background for about six weeks. The first result completes a calculation set out in 2003: whether the Hawking–Page transition — the abrupt, boiling-style switch between a hot gas and a black hole that Hawking and Page found in 1983 — stays abrupt in the quantum regime where general relativity breaks down. Claude concluded it does, hinging on the sign of a three-loop coefficient nobody had computed in two decades, with nearly every number obtained two independent ways and an outside physicist reproducing the same answer by a different method. The second pins the exact optimal ceiling on the lightest black hole in a three-dimensional toy universe under the simplest "modular bootstrap" test — about c/9.1266, versus a known range of roughly 8.5 to 12 — via a spectral-curve method a parallel agent developed after reproducing a sphere-packing result OpenAI's model had proved; the results were verified in Lean in September. The caveats hold: the papers are labeled "primarily AI-generated," are not peer-reviewed, and Anthropic itself says neither result settles quantum gravity or even comes close.

## METR's Q4 audit: on its coding tasks, evaluators struggle to find humans who beat the models

The evaluation nonprofit METR released its Q4 2026 capability update Nov 20, covering public models through Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra, and its center of gravity is blunt: on METR's coding and optimization tasks, models "usually match or outperform skilled humans," and when the group recruited its own human baselines it "typically failed to find humans that outperform the current frontier models" — though where ten-plus specialists had attempted a task, models land at a very high percentile without quite reaching the top. Across the combined TH1.1/TH2 suites — the new TH2 layer adds optimization, judgment, continual-learning and research tasks — the task length models complete half the time doubles roughly every 3.6 months, echoing OpenAI's recent internal figure. Two measurement wrinkles matter: doubling a task's "lifecycle cost" — what it takes to turn it into a reinforcement-learning environment — hurts agents by roughly the equivalent of halving their time horizon, yet progress rates hold on the expensive tasks; and on judgment calls without fast empirical feedback, agents remain clearly worse than human experts, with model judges flattering outputs from similarly strong or stronger models. METR's composite index shows no accelerating slope across the public frontier, but the headline is the uncertainty it refuses to rule out: current evidence is insufficient to exclude "drastic AI R&D acceleration in the next couple of years."

## Thinking Machines' Inkling 2 Mini loops its middle layers to out-code bigger open models on a laptop

Mira Murati's Thinking Machines Lab released Inkling 2 Mini Nov 20 with open weights: a 26.4B-parameter mixture-of-experts where 3.4B parameters activate per token, but the middle two blocks of layers execute three times each, giving 5.4B effective weight activations — computation spent thinking inside the weights rather than writing long chains of thought. On the benchmarks TML reports, it leads every open-weights model under 100B parameters: DeepSWE v1.1 at 48.2% (Qwen3.8-27B: 42.2%), SWE-bench Pro v2 Hard at 77.9%, Vals Index v2.2 at 46.5%, BrowseComp at 79.0% with compaction. The monitorability experiment is the research payload: on a variable-loop variant released alongside, the no-chain-of-thought time horizon rose from 16 seconds at one pass to 37 at three, a serial-reasoning benchmark kept improving out to ten loops — twice the training depth — while the visible reasoning got shorter but stayed legible, and agentic-sabotage tests showed no upward trend. Practically: a ~20GB footprint trained natively in NVFP4, runnable on a 32GB Mac via Ollama (the team demoed it building a playable flight simulator offline on a MacBook Pro mid-flight), and shipped across vLLM, SGLang and Tinker. The honest limit: looping buys reasoning, not knowledge — factuality benchmarks stay flat against same-size models, and TML says the model covers that gap with search and tools.

## Tencent opens the weights of Hy4 — a 770B-parameter MoE with DeepSeek-style sparse attention

Two days after open-sourcing the smaller Hy4 Flash, Tencent on Nov 20 released the open weights of Hy4, its natively multimodal flagship: 770B total parameters with 49B active per token plus a 1.23B vision encoder, a 1M-token context, and the instruct model, an FP8 variant and the pre-trained base all downloadable from Hugging Face, ModelScope and GitCode. The architecture assembles techniques the open-weights world has already validated: 78 layers with one dense and 77 mixture-of-experts layers (256 routed experts, top-8 plus one shared per token), Gated DeepSeek Sparse Attention with an IndexCache that selects only the top 2,048 tokens per step — Tencent credits DeepSeek and GLM for the inspiration — and a built-in 3.3B "DFly2" drafter for speculative decoding that reuses Hy4's own cache. vLLM and SGLang shipped day-one support, Together AI hosted the model the same day, it is on OpenRouter and Tencent Cloud, and the API runs $0.834 per million input and $2.501 per million output tokens. A system card with safety, security and alignment evaluations — including cyber and biological risk reviews — shipped alongside the weights.

## OpenAI puts its production voice model in the API and bills silence at a discount

GPT-Live-1.1 — the full-duplex voice model that has powered ChatGPT Plus and Pro voice since Oct. 15 — landed in the API on Nov 19, giving developers the model that speaks while it listens and delegates heavy reasoning to a backend model of their choice without breaking conversational flow. OpenAI says it improves pronunciation and accent consistency across languages, transcription, understanding of precise details like letters and numbers, instruction following and task reliability. The developer-friendly addition is Standby: set an idle timeout from 5 to 60 seconds and the model pauses inference during silence while the WebRTC call stays open, resuming when someone speaks — billed at $0.02 per minute idle versus $0.05 active, a cheap fix for voice agents that spend much of their life waiting. The system card reports voice-safety evaluations at parity with predecessors in ChatGPT, while the API model scores numerically higher on mental-health, emotional-reliance and self-harm evals.
