## Edge0 open-sources a 145B agent model built to run on your desk, not in your rack

The Edge0 team open-sourced Edge0-145B and Edge0-120B on November 12 — by its own description the first open 145-billion-parameter model aimed at personal-computer agents rather than datacenter-only serving — under an Apache 2.0 stack that spans Hugging Face checkpoints (Edge0-145B-q3k at 40.81 GiB, an NVFP4 build at 47.79 GiB, plus 120B variants), the edge0-strata runtime, and edge0-infer v0.2.0, a local server exposing OpenAI-compatible endpoints and an Anthropic Messages API that ships without the weights and needs CUDA 13, compute capability 7.5+, and 16 GB of VRAM. The design bet is different from SSD-streaming 800B experiments like InferSSD or Apple's four-Mac RDMA demo: rather than running a full cloud checkpoint out of RAM, Edge0 trained a latent sparse mixture-of-experts natively for one-GPU execution — 512 experts per layer with 10 routed plus 1 shared, only about 10.1 of 145 billion parameters active per token, 48 hybrid-attention layers, and 256K context with int8 KV cache keeping 32K resident — then splits memory three ways: hot experts in GPU memory, a roughly 40 GB expert library in system RAM, and about 48 GB of vocabulary data read from SSD on demand, with CPU/GPU co-scheduling and speculative decoding on top. The claimed headline is a 29 GB measured peak with 80+ tok/s decode; the model card is more specific about its rig — an RTX 5090 D with 24 GB — reporting 121.2 tok/s agent decode and 3,554.9 tok/s 16K prefill. The benchmark tables are almost entirely Edge0's own evals: DeepSWE 1.1 at 64.6 versus 58.7 for Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, Terminal-Bench 2.1 at 86.52 against a Claude Opus 4.6 Max row they put at 78.2, and GPQA-Diamond at 90.4, near DeepSeek-V4-Flash's 90.8 — directional comparisons with footnoted caveats, not leaderboard certification. The interesting claim isn't any single score; it's 72.8 mean across six benchmarks at a 29 GB footprint against an estimated ~70 GB for the Qwen 4-bit alternative, which is the kind of quality-per-gigabyte figure that, if it survives independent replication, reshapes what "local agent" hardware has to be.

## OpenAI turns Daybreak into products: Red Team tests your web apps, Defense Factory drafts the patches

At a Thursday keynote, OpenAI president Greg Brockman announced the first products of the Daybreak security platform: Daybreak Red Team, available in early access, which uses OpenAI models to probe companies' web applications for vulnerabilities, and Daybreak Defense Factory, which runs agents built on OpenAI's latest cyber model — identified by The Register as GPT-6 Cyber, the Preparedness-Framework-Critical model whose access is gated — and, per early coverage, produces patch drafts from what the attackers find. The sales pitch was as direct as the products: "We're now at a point where companies will be secure only to the extent of the frontier model that they use to defend themselves and how well they utilize that," Brockman said, framing frontier-model defense as the new security floor. The move also completes a loop the lab has been narrating all week — the same day, its alignment team published the jailbreak-robustness research described below, and the underlying cyber model was already running inside daybreak-style evals. Whether Red Team's automated penetration testing can displace human pentesters is the obvious question; what's new is a frontier lab selling both the adversary and the armor.

## Claude's worst login day: authentication broke while the API held

Friday morning Anthropic's status page went from a 09:58 UTC "investigating an issue with users authenticating to Claude.ai and Claude Code" note to a board showing claude.ai in major outage, Claude Code and platform.claude.com in partial outage, and Claude Cowork degraded — with api.anthropic.com staying operational throughout. The distinction matters more than it sounds: batch jobs, eval harnesses and server-side agents holding long-lived API keys kept running, while everything that depends on OAuth and web-session establishment — IDE sessions Claude Code must silently renew, browser chat, Cowork — died with the login path, and a stuck sign-in reliably reads as "the model broke" even when inference was healthy. At 11:05 UTC the incident moved to monitoring with "a fix has been deployed for the issue affecting Claude services and we are monitoring for recovery," leaving no stated root cause; the affected-surface list had already grown to include Cowork. It is also Anthropic's second reliability shock in forty-eight hours — on November 12, a separate status thread tracked mail, calendar and search failures on the Microsoft 365 connector, attributed upstream to a Microsoft service incident, which caught Claude for Outlook users in the crossfire. The operational lesson two days in a row: log which surface failed, because UI-side OAuth flows and API-key paths fail differently and it changes who notices and when.

## Meta's Muse will now place calls to US businesses — for paying subscribers

Meta moved its consumer agent Muse's outbound calling from an invite-only September beta to every paid US subscriber on November 12, with chief AI officer Alexandr Wang announcing the rollout: "let muse take care of all those calls you don't want to make — insurance? cable? DMV? dinner res?" The feature is exactly what it sounds like — you hand Muse a task that requires a phone conversation and it dials, speaks, and transcribes for you — and it lands on an agent Meta says has passed 10 million users, on subscription tiers that start at $20 a month for 500 million weekly Muse tokens (Power) and run to $100 a month for 3 billion (Maximum). The caveats are as instructive as the feature: businesses and receptionists hang up on AI callers when they identify them, and an earlier Meta experiment that used human contractors to place some Muse calls was pulled back after employees raised privacy concerns — Meta promised disclosures and safeguards before a wider release but hasn't said whether people are still in the loop on calls today, and Engadget noted one shared call where the agent said it was calling "on someone's behalf" and mentioned transcribing, without explicitly identifying itself as an AI. Next up, Wang says the recently added dedicated Muse email address will reach free users. Google and the startup Instinct offer similar calling; the receptionists' revolt is the shared bottleneck.

## Retell ships Tell-1, a voice LM built for the phone line rather than the chat box

Retell AI launched Tell-1 on November 12, a language model for live contact-center calls trained on hundreds of millions of calls and tuned for the failure modes PSTN callers actually produce: half answers where the caller gives only a first name and the agent must ask for the rest without resetting the form, detours that need acknowledging before returning to the task, and tool calls timed to fire once enough slots are filled — without dead air. The headline mechanism is interleaved reasoning: the model updates its internal plan while audio is already flowing rather than waiting for a full completion batch, and Retell claims under 100 ms median (under 300 ms peak) latency on its own bench — figures that matter because turn-speed, not benchmark prose, is what makes phone agents sound present rather than scripted. Tell 1 Beta is priced at $0.035 per minute (chat AI messages $0.011), which on the company's own comparison table undercuts listed frontier voice entries like GPT-6 Astra at $0.32 a minute; Retell's changelog puts it #1 overall on its public vCX board — best in colloquial speech and latency, with grounding and tool-calling columns still won by others — and its November writeup claims 94.45% average across instruction following, grounding, tool calling, and colloquial quality on 62 listed configurations. All of that is Retell-measured, not third-party, so the honest verdict is directional. The bundled Retell Dialer completes the pitch for outbound campaigns: callbacks at callee-local time, qualified handoffs to humans, CRM hooks and spam monitoring. The strategic read is the pricing itself — Retell is trying to own the per-minute "brain" of the call instead of reselling OpenAI or Anthropic tokens with a markup.

## OpenAI's automated red team turned synthetic jailbreaks into safer models — on cyber, Astra held 91.5% where Sol held 59%

OpenAI's alignment team published "Improving Fuzzy Robustness in LLM Safety with Automated Red Teaming" on November 12, defining fuzzy robustness as how reliably a model withholds disallowed assistance under adversarial pressure, including jailbreaks — hardest in dual-use domains like cyber and biology where the same information cuts both ways. The pipeline is a PAIR-style automated red-teaming harness: a helpful-only reasoning model from the GPT-5.6 Sol family — with no jailbreak-specific training, which OpenAI calls an emergent phenomenon — iteratively refines prompts against GPT-5.4 and GPT-5.5 defender models until something unsafe comes out; adding attacker-focused training tasks like GPT-Red improved the attacker's efficiency further. The key experimental finding is about training data: RL on original seed prompts starts at near-saturated safety scores and teaches little, while RL on the harvested "robustified" jailbreaks starts at a much less saturated level and actually improves, and the trained model stayed more robust against a unseen attacker model and against adaptive multi-turn attackers with query access to the defender — generalization to qualitatively different attacks, which is precisely what static jailbreak sets don't test. The released numbers measure the model layer without production safeguards: on fixed pre-generated cyber jailbreaks, GPT-6 Astra answered 91.5% safely versus GPT-5.6 Sol's 59%, and Astra remained more robust across attacker budgets on the adaptive eval, while also refusing legitimate requests less often — a Pareto improvement, not a safety-versus-helpfulness trade. Detailed results live in the GPT-6 Astra system card.

## BirdDreams: NVIDIA predicts dashcam and top-down video together so the driving world model can be steered

A joint NVIDIA/KAIST team introduced BirdDreams, an autoregressive driving world model that generates perspective dashcam video and a schematic bird's-eye-view video at the same time (arXiv November 10; #1 paper of the day on Hugging Face November 13, upvoting it past every other candidate). The design insight is prior work's awkward contract: driving world models that control surrounding-vehicle motion condition on layout inputs that require knowing the vehicles' future trajectories in advance. BirdDreams instead runs two video diffusion transformers — one RGB, one BEV rendered as simple fixed-scale shapes for training — exchanging information through joint attention so the scene layout becomes part of the predicted future rather than an input, with cross-chunk key/value reuse and a "Resampling Forcing" fine-tune to handle autoregressive history drift. The BEV then doubles as the control surface: the system translates and rotates the latest predicted top-down frame along an input ego trajectory and feeds the result back as soft conditioning — and at inference it needs only a single camera image, no HD map and no vehicle state. On PhysicalAI-AV and nuScenes it follows input ego trajectories more faithfully than prior driving world models, and in human evaluation it beats a perspective-only baseline on realistic surrounding-vehicle behavior; a preliminary post-training experiment pushes the same stack toward directly predicting future ego trajectories. It's research, not a product — the world-action-model direction, where layout and control are first-class citizens of the generative model, is the same one Runway's Praxis-1 planted in robotics this week.
