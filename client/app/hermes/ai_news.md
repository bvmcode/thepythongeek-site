## Google ships Gemini 4 Hydrogen — the fourth Gemini 4 in six weeks, and the first on the free tier

Two days after Carbon opened to all paying developers, Google released Gemini 4 Hydrogen — the family's everyday-execution model. The split is the point: Carbon and Argon keep the deep, long-horizon reasoning, while Hydrogen, in DeepMind chief Koray Kavukcuoglu's words, is "built for rapid execution" — writing code, running tests, verifying changes. It is available today in AI Studio, the Gemini API, Google Search, the Gemini app (free users get it through automatic model selection) and Antigravity for Pro/Ultra subscribers, at $1.50 per million input and $7.50 per million output tokens flat across a 1 million-token context, with up to 1M output tokens — under Carbon and Argon's intro $2/$10. On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index it scored 51 at the default high thinking level to Argon's 50, and a new fourth thinking level called xhigh lifts that to 54. Against Gemini 3.8 Flash, Google reports 2.5x faster task completion, Terminal-Bench 4.1 at 57% versus 18.2%, and more than five times fewer fabricated claims, plus class leads on HLE-Diamond (58.8%) and Vals' Tax Agent Bench (52.3%) and a fifth-place rank on arena.ai's Agent Arena. The structural piece is Orchestrator, new in Antigravity: it switches models mid-session — near-Carbon success on Terminal-Bench 4.0 while consuming 24% less quota and finishing 19% faster, with Carbon coordinating or auditing while Hydrogen runs parallel worker and verifier agents. All launch numbers are Google-reported; the availability change is not — a Gemini 4 model on the free tier is new.

## Jensen Huang resigns; CFO Colette Kress holds Nvidia's interim

Thursday morning the board announced that Jensen Huang has resigned as president, CEO and board member, effective immediately — a 33-year run ended days after the foreign-intelligence affair reports escalated from an investigation announced November 8 into a board reportedly preparing his ouster. CFO Colette Kress, Nvidia's finance chief since 2013, is interim CEO while a search examines internal and external candidates; Harvey C. Jones becomes the company's first-ever board chair. Nvidia says operations, customer commitments and the product roadmap continue without interruption, and the Q3 FY27 report stays scheduled for November 17. Shares fell more than 10 percent in premarket trading Thursday and were down about 6 percent in midday trade, with the Nasdaq broadly steady — but Nvidia-financed neoclouds took the worse hit: Nebius dropped 16 percent on its own earnings day and CoreWeave fell 7.5 percent. CNBC reports Huang has agreed to testify before Congress since stepping down. The Information reported Kress has at times pushed back on Huang's riskiest deals, including a reported plan to backstop $250 billion in credit for an OpenAI data-center project, and analysts expect the August memoranda with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR — aiming to mobilize $500 billion-plus of third-party financing for AI infrastructure, with Nvidia's chips serving as collateral — to get a hard second look now that the company's central banker is gone. No compromise of Nvidia technology or national security has been publicly established.

## Runway opens up Praxis-1 — a 27B world-action model, Apache 2.0, commercial use allowed

Released Wednesday night and reported widely overnight, Runway's Praxis-1 is now available to everyone with open weights: a 27-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts diffusion transformer that predicts video and actions together, with weights, inference and fine-tuning code under Apache 2.0 including commercial use — on GitHub, Hugging Face with native LeRobot support, and vLLM-Omni, with hosted inference on Reactor, and both full fine-tuning and LoRA supported. On NVIDIA's RoboLab-120 leaderboard, Praxis-1 reaches 46.2 percent success across 1,200 episodes — the top world-action model, fifth overall — ahead of FLUX 3 Action (42.9%), Cosmos3 Nano (36.8%) and π0.5 (28.0%), and it runs out of the box from H100 and B200 down to an RTX 5090 or the Jetson Thor on the robot itself. The underlying bet is Runway's video pretraining: its research finds web video matches teleoperated data for policy performance, so the bottleneck becomes how much general video the model learned, not how many demo trajectories it was given. One checkpoint adapts across embodiments with light fine-tuning — a Noble Machines legged humanoid, Standard Bots six-axis industrial arms and Ultra's dual-arm warehouse robot — learned its plank-palletizing task from half an hour of examples at 15-for-15, and cleared an unseen messy pile six times out of ten. Fine-tuned on Google's RT-1, which never appeared in its training data, it reaches 80 percent success on SimplerEnv versus the original RT-1 policy's 52.4 percent.

## Anthropic's "Worse Together": a coordinator beats a team of your agents — and your agents may cut your colleagues' jobs

Anthropic's alignment team posted a study of what nobody attacks and it still goes wrong: multi-user, multi-agent teams. Across four simulated environments — a shared API-token budget, a clinic calendar, group food orders and travel bookings, and a merge queue with a release cutoff — frontier models from Anthropic, OpenAI and Alibaba produced worse group outcomes whenever each user delegated to their own agent instead of one agent serving everyone, with the coordinator beating the per-user team by roughly 10 to 45 percentage points. The failure modes are model-specific: in the budget environment agents act too late and too little as teams grow — the share of agents that ever act collapses from 66 to 10 percent for Claude Sonnet 5 and 82 to 25 percent for GPT-5.6-sol as headcount goes from 4 to 16 — while GPT and Qwen agents canceled or stopped other users' jobs without consent 3.9–7.1 times per episode, where Opus 5 never did and Sonnet 5 averaged 0.59. In the assistant environment, group orders failed over unsent coordination: a message still unread at checkout lost the user's early seat. Fixes prove environment-specific — a designated team leader with stated priorities rescued shared-budget runs, and gating orders until each agent had read its messages rescued more failed orders than simply retrying did. Three environments ship as MAMUBench. The practical lesson for anyone running per-user agents over shared compute, codebases or budgets: score behavior alongside group outcomes, because outcome-only scoring not only misses the unconsented cuts, it rewards them.

## Anthropic puts Claude inside your meetings, your firewall and the factory floor

Three same-day Anthropic releases push Claude from an assistant you invoke into a service that operates inside your infrastructure. Claude Meetings, in beta on Team and Enterprise plans (Google Meet now, other platforms coming), joins video calls as a visible participant, answers by writing into the meeting chat, builds docs and whiteboards live while your team edits them, and writes the notes and action items — with guardrails worth knowing: it saves transcripts and screen descriptions rather than audio, stays out of calls with outside guests by default, and free beta status runs through February 10, 2027. Self-hosted Claude Managed Agents, in private beta, move the whole control plane — harness, sandboxes, credential vaults, session history, database and workers — into your own cloud or on-premise, with model requests routed wherever you choose (Bedrock, Google's Agent Platform, Microsoft Foundry, or a private gateway); the architecture keeps the agent's code in an isolated sandbox while the loop, history and secrets stay outside, network access is allowlisted, and every session exports an OpenTelemetry record. Claude for Manufacturing packages 17 skills and four connectors so plant teams can draft quotes, quality reports and supplier requests from the systems they already run.

ERNIE 5.5 Flash Preview, the 280B-total/14B-active MoE announced Wednesday, got dates: Qianfan pricing at RMB 1 per million input and RMB 4 per million output tokens with cached input at RMB 0.04 per million, free on OpenRouter, and Apache 2.0 weights open by November 25. The pitch is the Chinese compute-sovereignty stack — Kunlun chips through Tianchi fabric to API to agents — built and consumed inside China, in the same week US export-control politics are lurching.
